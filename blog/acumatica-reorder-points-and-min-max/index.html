Acumatica Reorder Points and Min/Max

How to configure Acumatica inventory reorder points, min/max stock levels, safety stock, and the replenishment engine that creates POs automatically.

John KihiuNov 20, 202512 min read

Min/max and reorder points are the settings customers ask about first and revisit least. They live on the Item Warehouse Details tab of the stock item — one Replenishment Source, one Replenishment Method, a Min and a Max — and once they are typed in during go-live, nobody looks at them again until the warehouse is either sitting on six months of dead stock or explaining a stockout to a customer. Neither is a bug. Both are the predictable result of static settings applied to demand that is not static.

Where the settings actually live

Replenishment configuration sits on the Item Warehouse Details record for a stock item at a specific warehouse — the same place you set costing and valuation per site. Each combination of item and warehouse gets its own Replenishment Source (Purchased, Manufactured, or Transferred from another warehouse), its own Replenishment Method, and its own set of quantities. This matters because the same SKU can be min/max in one branch warehouse and reorder-point-driven in a distribution center, and that is intentional — a fast-moving regional warehouse and a slow consignment site should not share a replenishment strategy just because they share an item.

Replenishment Method has three real options. None means the system will never suggest a purchase or transfer for that item at that warehouse — someone has to plan it manually, which is correct for one-off or engineered-to-order items. Min/Max is the simple threshold model. Reorder Point pulls in lead time and usage to size a buffer instead of a flat number.

Min/Max: the simple case

Min/Max is a threshold, not a forecast. When the available quantity — on-hand plus on-order, minus allocations — drops to or below Min, the replenishment engine suggests an order that brings the item back up to Max. That is the entire model. It does not know about lead time, it does not know about seasonality, and it does not care whether last month's usage was double this month's.

That simplicity is a feature for the right items. Stable, low-variability SKUs — the ones that sell roughly the same quantity every week — do not need a lead-time-aware buffer; a well-tuned Min/Max pair gets the reorder timing close enough, and it is trivial for a planner to sanity-check at a glance. The failure mode shows up on items that are not stable: a Min set for average demand gets blown through in a single large order, and the warehouse is short until the next PO lands.

Reorder Point and safety stock

Reorder Point-based replenishment is the same trigger mechanic — order when available quantity hits the threshold — but the threshold itself is calculated instead of typed in by feel. It factors in Lead Time (days), average daily usage, and a Safety Stock quantity that absorbs variability in either demand or the vendor's actual delivery time. If you're using EOQ (Economic Order Quantity) instead of a fixed Max, the suggested order quantity is sized to balance ordering cost against carrying cost rather than just topping up to a round number.

This method earns its complexity on items where demand is genuinely variable, or where the vendor's lead time is long enough that a stockout during replenishment is expensive. Safety stock is the part people under-tune: set it too low and you have reinvented Min/Max with extra steps; set it too high and you are carrying inventory that only protects against a demand spike that never comes.

MRP versus manual requirement planning

Neither Min/Max nor Reorder Point settings do anything by themselves — they are inputs that a planning process reads. Customers running the Material Requirements Planning module get purchase and production suggestions generated by MRP Preview and MRP Recalculation, which net demand across sales orders, forecasts, and existing supply before applying these thresholds. Customers not on MRP typically work from the simpler Purchase Requirements and Transfer Requirements inquiries, which react more directly to the Min/Max or reorder point numbers without the multi-level netting.

The practical difference matters when you're diagnosing why a suggested PO looks wrong. On MRP, a suggestion can be suppressed or resized because of a sales order pegged against existing supply, not because the replenishment settings are broken. On the simpler inquiries, what you see is closer to a direct read of the threshold math.

Stale settings are the real defect, not the formula

Min/Max and reorder point values set once at go-live and never revisited are the single most common cause of chronic stockouts or overstock I see in the field. Demand patterns shift, a vendor's lead time changes after a supplier switch, and nobody updates the item record to match. Put a recurring review on the calendar — quarterly for A items, less often for C items — rather than trusting numbers that were correct eighteen months ago.

Tying it to ABC classification

Not every item deserves the same level of tuning attention. High-value, high-velocity A items are worth the effort of reorder-point-with-safety-stock, reviewed frequently, because a stockout is expensive and overstock ties up real capital. C items — low value, low velocity — can usually tolerate a loose Min/Max buffer reviewed once or twice a year; the carrying cost of being slightly wrong is trivial. Applying the same review cadence to both is wasted effort in one direction and negligence in the other.

A generic inquiry over INSiteStatus joined to INItemSiteSettings is a fast way to surface items where on-hand plus on-order has dropped below the reorder point or Min, without waiting for the next MRP run:

SQL · REPLENISHMENT SHORTAGE CHECK
SELECT
    iss.InventoryID,
    iss.SiteID,
    iss.QtyOnHand,
    iss.QtyOnOrder,
    (iss.QtyOnHand + iss.QtyOnOrder - iss.QtyAllocated) AS AvailableQty,
    ic.ReplenishmentMethod,
    ic.MinQty,
    ic.MaxQty,
    ic.ReorderPoint,
    ic.SafetyStock,
    ic.LeadTime
FROM INSiteStatus iss
INNER JOIN INItemSiteSettings ic
    ON ic.InventoryID = iss.InventoryID
   AND ic.SiteID = iss.SiteID
WHERE ic.ReplenishmentMethod IN ('M', 'R')
  AND (iss.QtyOnHand + iss.QtyOnOrder - iss.QtyAllocated)
      <= CASE ic.ReplenishmentMethod
             WHEN 'M' THEN ic.MinQty
             ELSE ic.ReorderPoint
         END
ORDER BY AvailableQty ASC;

Treat the field and table names as illustrative — verify the actual DAC and generic-inquiry field mappings in your build before wiring this into a dashboard, since exact naming can shift slightly by version.
